# Financial Statement Analysis — Ratio Summary

## AAPL (Apple)
Apple's Current Ratio sits below 1 (0.87–0.99) across all years, which would 
normally signal short-term liquidity risk. However, this isn't a red flag for 
Apple specifically given its massive cash reserves and ability to raise capital 
instantly. More notably, ROE is unusually high (150–190%) — this is largely 
driven by aggressive stock buybacks shrinking the equity base, not purely 
operational strength. Overall: financially healthy, but ratios here need 
context rather than face-value reading.

## MSFT (Microsoft)
Microsoft shows the strongest overall profile in this comparison. Net profit 
margin climbed steadily from ~34% (2023) to ~40% (2026), the clearest upward 
trend of any company analyzed. Debt-to-equity is low and falling (0.99 → 0.71), 
meaning the company relies less on debt over time while still growing. Current 
ratio stays comfortably above 1 throughout, indicating no liquidity concerns. 
Overall: financially healthy and improving — the strongest fundamentals in 
this group.

## PG (Procter & Gamble)
Procter & Gamble is stable but unremarkable. Net profit margin holds in a tight 
band (~17.7%–18.9%) with no clear upward or downward trend, and debt-to-equity 
gradually declines (1.58 → 1.34), suggesting slow, steady deleveraging. Current 
ratio sits below 1 across all years (0.63–0.73), lower than AAPL's, but this is 
also typical for a mature consumer goods company with predictable cash flow 
rather than a liquidity warning sign. Overall: consistent and low-risk, but 
without the growth trajectory MSFT shows.

## XOM (ExxonMobil)
ExxonMobil shows the clearest weakening trend in this group. Net profit margin 
fell from ~14% (2022) to ~9% (2025), and ROE dropped even more sharply, from 
~29% to ~11% over the same period. Debt-to-equity stayed relatively low and 
fairly stable (0.85 → 0.70), so this isn't a leverage problem — it looks more 
like margin compression. This is confirmed by industry-wide data: combined 
profits across major oil companies fell 16% in 2024 as Brent crude averaged 
below $80/barrel (down from $83 in 2023), and ExxonMobil's own Q4 earnings 
specifically cited lower crude prices and weaker refining margins as the 
cause (Anadolu Agency, Channels TV). This confirms XOM's decline is sector-wide 
cyclicality tied to oil prices, not a company-specific weakness — worth 
monitoring if oil prices recover.
